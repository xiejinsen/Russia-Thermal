## Q1 — Problem and target mapping

The paper compares pool-boiling performance on aluminum alloy, copper and WC-coated copper surfaces after polishing or nanosecond-laser treatment.

Laser processing creates micro-finned and anisotropic / multimodal hierarchical roughness; textured samples are then hydrophobized.

Degassed distilled water is used as the boiling fluid.

This makes the paper materially closer to the copper-water thermal-management target than TPU's droplet or boiler studies, but it remains open atmospheric-style pool boiling rather than a sealed VC.

## Q2 — Novelty vs strong baseline

The useful contribution is multi-material comparison plus surface-function aging under prolonged heat-flux exposure.

The study does more than record a contact angle:
- bubble characteristics are visualized;
- HTC / CHF are measured;
- results are compared with established models;
- functional-property evolution after prolonged thermal exposure is assessed.

However, laser-textured copper boiling surfaces are already a heavily populated global field, and China has actual UTVC surface-modification evidence.

## Q3 — Falsifiable hypothesis

Nanosecond-laser-created microfins / multimodal roughness plus controlled wetting state can alter nucleation-site population, bubble dynamics and liquid replenishment enough to improve pool-boiling performance, with some surface functions surviving prolonged thermal exposure.

The phone-transfer hypothesis is much stricter:
the same or a related spatial function must improve confined rewetting in a sealed sub-mm copper-water VC.

The paper does not test that.

## Q4 — Research lineage / competing route

This work extends TPU's laser heat-transfer-surface line into:
- copper;
- water;
- boiling;
- long heat-flux exposure.

It therefore strengthens TPU's process / thermal-experiment credibility.

But global and China literature already includes:
- laser-textured copper pool boiling;
- biphilic copper surfaces;
- UTVC laser-wick treatment;
- oxidation / corrosion / laser comparison in assembled UTVCs.

The residual cannot be generic boiling enhancement.

## Q6 — Experiment / method design

Samples are prepared by polishing or nanosecond-laser processing.

Laser processing forms microfinned / hierarchical textured surfaces, which are subsequently hydrophobized.

Pool boiling in degassed distilled water is observed before boiling-crisis onset.

The study records bubble behavior, determines HTC and CHF, compares experimental behavior with known prediction models, and assesses evolution of functional surface properties under prolonged heat loading.

The accessible preprint / institutional record does not expose a decision-safe full table of per-surface HTC / CHF values in the current review.

## Q7 — Quantitative evidence / reproducibility

The strongest decision-safe result recovered in this pass is structural rather than a single performance number:

- copper is directly included;
- degassed distilled water is used;
- both HTC and CHF are measured;
- prolonged heat-flux exposure is explicitly used to assess functional-property evolution.

Because exact per-texture numerical HTC / CHF values were not independently recovered, this card does not invent a percentage improvement.

## Q8 — What it proves / does not prove

It supports:

- TPU can laser-process copper for boiling experiments;
- TPU can measure bubble / HTC / CHF response;
- TPU considers functional-property evolution under prolonged heat exposure;
- TPU has a credible copper-water heat-transfer process bridge.

It does not prove:

- sub-150 μm functional geometry;
- sealed-vacuum compatibility;
- VC wick capillary improvement;
- spatial routing / rewetting advantage;
- superiority over China UTVC laser / oxidation / chemical processes;
- long-term sealed-device wetting retention.

## Q9 — Decision contribution / control point

This is the best remaining TPU paper for keeping a Stage-0 process test alive.

It closes one earlier uncertainty — copper applicability — and partially supports thermal durability competence.

But it does not close the actual differentiation question.

Therefore the TPU challenger should not be promoted; its only rational use is a bounded falsification experiment asking whether TPU can translate its copper laser/wetting control into a sealed, confined function that strong uniform references cannot match.

## Q10 — Next action / promotion or kill gate

Run one function-first Stage-0 experiment with four controls:

1. untreated wick / copper;
2. optimized uniform laser treatment;
3. strong non-laser superhydrophilic treatment;
4. TPU-inspired spatial pattern.

Require:
- phone-relevant copper thickness;
- low outgassing;
- post-bake wetting retention;
- sealed-fluid compatibility;
- confined rewetting / dryout benefit.

If branch 4 does not provide repeatable incremental benefit, downgrade the entire TPU challenger to HOLD / reference-only.
