Title:
Hydrophobization of Metal Surfaces by Laser Treatment and Subsequent Heat Treatment of Hydrocarbon Liquids

Authors:
D.V. Feoktistov; E.G. Orlova; G.E. Kotelnikov; K.K. Paushkina; A.O. Pleshko; O.V. Semyonov; D.O. Glushkov; A.V. Buglak

Direct reported results:
- a two-step process combines nanosecond-laser multimodal micro/nanoscale roughness with low-temperature thermolysis products from hydrocarbon-containing liquids;
- CH2 / CH3-containing surface groups are identified as the hydrophobizing chemistry;
- SEM / AFM characterize morphology and roughness; XPS / FTIR characterize surface chemistry;
- multiple carbon-containing liquids, including waste oils, can serve as hydrophobization feedstock;
- chemical-resistance and oscillating-sand abrasion tests show retained superhydrophobicity on AlMg3 alloy after aggressive exposure.

Decision use:
strong TPU process-durability evidence, but the hydrocarbon-derived functional layer creates a mandatory contamination / outgassing gate for sealed two-phase-device transfer.

Boundary:
does not prove vacuum outgassing, sealed-fluid compatibility, copper-specific behavior, post-degassing wetting retention or confined capillary benefit.
