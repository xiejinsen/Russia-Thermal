## Q1 — Problem and target mapping

The paper addresses how a sealed copper-water vapor chamber can lose heat-transfer performance even when gross shell, seal and visible structural damage is not the primary failure signature.

This maps directly to the phone-VC reliability problem because chemical aging of a copper wick can degrade capillary behavior before an obvious mechanical defect appears.

## Q2 — Novelty vs strong baseline

For this project the important novelty is the mechanistic chain:

oxygen / oxidation state → copper-wick surface change → wetting / capillary degradation → evaporation-performance loss.

The paper is therefore a strong product-path comparator to MPEI's long-duration hierarchical-surface aging evidence.

## Q3 — Falsifiable hypothesis

Residual or internal oxygen can chemically age a copper wick enough to degrade wettability and capillary pressure, producing thermal failure even without gross structural damage.

## Q4 — Capability lineage / competing route

The paper sits in a strong Chinese copper-water VC reliability and process-engineering context.

For this project it directly pressures any claim that long-duration Russian surface-aging experience by itself establishes a broader reliability advantage.

The relevant comparison is complementary rather than identical: MPEI contributes real-calendar hierarchical-surface aging; this paper contributes product-relevant copper-water chemical-failure mechanism.

## Q5 — Technical control variables

Relevant variables include:

- copper-water sealed VC;
- oxygen / oxidation state;
- wick-surface composition;
- wettability;
- capillary pressure;
- vacuum and process quality.

## Q6 — Experiment / method design

The historical decision card records comparison of normal and failed vapor chambers while examining:

- gross structure / leakage condition;
- wick surface composition;
- wetting / capillary state;
- thermal performance.

The current review basis remains publisher abstract / summary plus decision review, not a newly verified full-text extraction.

## Q7 — Quantitative evidence / reproducibility

The migrated card records approximately a three-percentage-point increase in wick oxygen fraction and a corresponding decrease in copper fraction in failed devices, together with wetting / capillary degradation.

Because the current project has not upgraded this pilot to a verified full-text review, these values remain inherited decision-card details and should be rechecked before being used as exact design inputs.

Conceptual reproducibility is stronger than process-specific reproducibility.

## Q8 — What it proves / does not prove

It supports:

- product-relevant copper-water VC chemical-aging research in China;
- oxygen / oxidation as a first-order reliability control variable;
- failure without obvious gross structural damage.

It does not prove:

- equivalence to MPEI's 42-month operation evidence;
- that oxidation is the only phone-VC degradation mode;
- a universal lifetime model for all ultra-thin VC designs.

## Q9 — Decision contribution / control point

This paper is primarily a comparator / pressure source.

It forces `DIR-HEALTH-AWARE-UTVC` to remain a narrow complementary thesis: any future health-aware model must distinguish capillary / wetting aging from oxygen-driven chemistry, fill-state drift and package/interface effects rather than treating MPEI's surface-aging signal as a unique reliability mechanism.

## Q10 — Next action / promotion or kill gate

Use this comparator to define mandatory chemistry / process controls in any future aging study:

- oxygen budget or equivalent process record;
- surface chemistry before / after aging;
- wetting / capillary retention;
- sealed-process context.

If the proposed Russia-informed health model cannot add information beyond these strong product-path reliability variables, it should not be promoted.
