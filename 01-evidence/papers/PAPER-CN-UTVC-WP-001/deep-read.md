## Q1 — Problem and target mapping

The paper studies how composite wick architecture and wettability-patterned surfaces affect heat transfer in 0.4 mm-thick UTVCs for portable electronics.

This is directly on the user's target class, unlike TPU's open-droplet / boiler / pool-boiling studies.

## Q2 — Novelty vs strong baseline

The paper fabricates and compares SWM, SWM-SM and SWM-WP branches inside completed 0.4 mm UTVCs.

For this project, that means spatial wettability patterning inside an actual ultra-thin sealed VC is already demonstrated independently in China.

## Q3 — Falsifiable hypothesis

Replacing part of the conventional screen-mesh liquid-spreading function with a patterned superhydrophilic bottom-sheet region can redistribute liquid while preserving vapor-channel area and improve UTVC performance under selected orientations.

## Q4 — Capability lineage / competing route

The paper sits inside a mature China composite-wick UTVC line covering:
- SWM / screen mesh;
- oxidation / laser surface treatment;
- fill ratio;
- orientation;
- sub-0.4 mm packaging.

TPU therefore cannot claim the generic spatial-wettability function as a differentiated control point.

## Q5 — Technical control variables

- total thickness: 0.4 mm;
- SWM architecture;
- SWM-SM branch;
- SWM-WP branch;
- normal versus enhanced SWM variants;
- wick structure;
- orientation;
- superhydrophilic patterned bottom surface.

## Q6 — Experiment / method design

Five 0.4 mm UTVC variants are fabricated:
S1 NSWM, S2 NSWM-WP, S3 NSWM-SM, S4 ESWM-WP, S5 ESWM-SM.

Their temperature distribution and heat-transfer performance are compared under multiple orientations.

## Q7 — Quantitative evidence / reproducibility

The publisher page confirms completed 0.4 mm devices and direct branch comparison.

The current accessible text does not expose a single decision-safe full performance table for every branch, so this card does not invent per-branch thermal-resistance or Qmax values.

The existence and target-system integration of SWM-WP are, however, fully sufficient for the differentiation question.

## Q8 — What it proves / does not prove

It proves:
- spatial wettability patterning can be integrated inside a 0.4 mm sealed UTVC;
- the concept is not uniquely available through TPU.

It does not prove:
- TPU cannot offer a better process;
- identical transient rewetting, durability, contamination or moving-hotspot behavior;
- production superiority.

## Q9 — Decision contribution / control point

This paper removes the last concept-level basis for keeping TPU as an active Stage-0 collaboration challenger.

After this comparator, TPU's remaining public residual is process execution / diagnostics, not a unique phone thermal function.

## Q10 — Next action / promotion or kill gate

Do not fund a TPU Stage-0 solely to test whether spatial wettability patterning can work in a thin VC; that question is already answered independently.

Reopen only if TPU can show a distinct, phone-relevant increment such as:
- demonstrably lower-outgassing / more durable patterned process;
- superior transient rewetting under matched geometry;
- process yield or diagnostic capability not available domestically.
