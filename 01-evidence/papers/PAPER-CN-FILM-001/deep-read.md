## Q1 — Problem and target mapping

The paper targets high-heat-flux electronics cooling using capillary-driven thin-film boiling.

The engineering problem is the classic wick tradeoff:
small pores raise capillary pressure but also raise flow resistance, while large pores improve permeability but weaken liquid pumping.

The proposed solution is a gradient porous wire-mesh wick.

## Q2 — Novelty vs strong baseline

The work combines different wire-mesh porosities into a gradient structure so capillary pressure and permeability can be separated spatially.

It is a strong comparator because the system obtains high CHF without the forced gas shear central to the Russian Extreme Film platform.

## Q3 — Falsifiable hypothesis

A gradient wick can outperform homogeneous wicks by balancing capillary pumping, liquid replenishment, vapor escape and bubble departure.

The experiment reports performance consistent with that hypothesis.

## Q4 — Research lineage / competing route

This is part of a strong China capillary-fed thin-film / wick-engineering line.

It competes with the Russian route on high heat flux and liquid-film supply, but not on gas-shear free-surface instability physics.

This distinction is important: the Russian residual cannot be "thin films cool electronics effectively" or "high CHF."

## Q6 — Experiment / method design

Four wick samples with different porosities are fabricated and tested with deionized water.

Homogeneous-wick capillary properties are analyzed theoretically.

Capillary-driven thin-film boiling experiments compare the wick configurations and also evaluate reduced-pressure effects on the gradient wick.

## Q7 — Data / reproducibility

The publisher record reports:

- CHF up to 202.8 W/cm²;
- maximum HTC reported as 121.9 kW/(m²·K) at 88.5 W/cm² in the abstract;
- gradient wick outperforms homogeneous designs.

The key engineering explanation is the improved balance between capillary force and permeability plus improved bubble separation / liquid replenishment.

## Q8 — Evidence vs hypothesis

- 12.5 μm slit-flow mapping;
