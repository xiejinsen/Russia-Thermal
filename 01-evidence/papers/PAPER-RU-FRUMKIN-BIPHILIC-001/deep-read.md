## Q1 — Problem and target mapping

The paper investigates saturated water pool boiling at atmospheric pressure on copper surfaces containing laser-ablated cavities that are selectively hydrophobized.

Its relevance is a surface-state control mechanism that can stabilize / activate nucleation sites.

It is not a shear-driven film experiment and not a phone vapor chamber.

## Q2 — Novelty vs strong baseline

The useful finding is that heat-transfer enhancement depends primarily on the specific density / pitch of hydrophobized cavities rather than their detailed shape and size.

Removing the hydrophobic treatment significantly reduces the enhancement.

That is a real surface-chemistry control result, but selective wettability and biphilic-surface concepts are already widely explored globally and in China.

## Q3 — Falsifiable hypothesis

Persistent selectively hydrophobic cavities can act as controlled vapor / nucleation sites and thereby intensify boiling, with cavity density / spacing acting as a stronger design variable than individual cavity geometry.

## Q4 — Research lineage / competing route

This work links:

- Kutateladze boiling / two-phase diagnostics;
- Frumkin surface chemistry;
- laser texturing;
- fluorinated-silane hydrophobization.

It demonstrates a useful cross-institution capability stack, but not a unique strategic control point because biphilic / patterned boiling surfaces are a crowded field.

## Q5 — Key mechanism / control point

Verified variables include:

- laser-ablated copper cavities;
- fluorinated methoxysilane chemisorption;
- hydrophobization at 105°C;
- cavity density / pitch;
- cavity shape / size;
- saturated water pool boiling;
- atmospheric pressure.

The reported dominant variable is hydrophobic-cavity specific density / pitch.

## Q6 — Experiment / method design

Copper surfaces are laser-textured to create cavities, then selectively hydrophobized by chemisorption of fluorinated methoxysilane vapor.

Boiling water experiments are run at atmospheric pressure and compared across cavity layouts and after removal of the water-repellent treatment.

The current review does not have the full numeric HTC / CHF table from the exact paper.

## Q7 — Data / reproducibility

Decision-safe reported findings:

- cavity density / pitch is the main parameter governing heat-transfer intensity;
- cavity shape and size have much smaller influence;
- removing hydrophobic treatment significantly reduces heat-transfer enhancement.

No exact enhancement percentage from this paper is added without full-text verification.

## Q8 — Evidence vs hypothesis

It supports:

- real Russian selective-wettability / surface-chemistry capability;
- a mechanistic role for spatially controlled nucleation sites.

It does not support:

- unique biphilic novelty;
- extreme-confinement free-surface film differentiation;
- phone-scale process durability;
- sealed-device performance.

## Q9 — Real decision contribution

This paper is supporting capability, not a reason to retain the Extreme Film reserve.

It shows that the Russian ecosystem can combine interfacial physics with surface chemistry, but the strategic residual remains shear-driven extreme-confinement instability / rupture physics.

## Q10 — Next action

Do not use this paper to justify a distinct collaboration direction.

If selective wettability is used in a future Stage-0 test, benchmark it against strong domestic / global biphilic controls and evaluate durability after realistic cleaning, sealing and aging.
