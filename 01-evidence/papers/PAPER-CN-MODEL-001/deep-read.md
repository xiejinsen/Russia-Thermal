## Q1 — Problem and target mapping

The paper studies stability and rupture of a three-dimensional evaporating / condensing liquid film falling down a heated or cooled incline.

The target is fundamental interfacial dynamics rather than UTVC design, but it is a strong comparator for the mathematical capability claim because it treats 3D nonlinear phase-change-film instability.

## Q2 — Novelty vs strong baseline

The paper derives a nonlinear long-wave evolution equation based on a Benney-type formulation and uses a more comprehensive phase-change boundary condition than the standard Hertz–Knudsen–Langmuir relation.

It explicitly includes:

- gravity;
- evaporation;
- vapor recoil;
- effective pressure;
- energy transport along the interface;
- 3D spanwise perturbations;
- nonlinear rupture evolution.

This is enough to reject any broad claim that current formal nonlinear evaporative-film mathematics is uniquely Russian.

## Q3 — Falsifiable hypothesis

Vapor recoil destabilizes an evaporating film, while effective pressure stabilizes it and can delay rupture; in 3D, spanwise perturbations can strengthen the destabilizing influence of vapor recoil.

The model also predicts distinct finite-time thinning scalings depending on the dominant mechanism.

## Q4 — Research lineage / competing route

The work sits in a modern China fluid-mechanics / heat-transfer line drawing on long-wave thin-film theory, interfacial phase-change boundary conditions and nonlinear dynamics.

It is methodologically different from the Russian exact/group-invariant school, but strategically that distinction alone is insufficient: both produce interpretable mechanism-level stability information.

The comparator therefore forces the Russian thesis to demonstrate practical leverage, not methodological uniqueness.

## Q5 — Key mechanism / control point

Key effects are:

- vapor recoil;
- effective pressure;
- gravity;
- evaporation;
- energy transport along the interface;
- streamwise and spanwise disturbances;
- nonlinear film-thickness evolution.

The paper separates how these terms affect growth rate, wave speed, stability and rupture.

## Q6 — Experiment / method design

A nonlinear long-wave equation is derived for a 3D evaporating / condensing falling film.

The authors perform:
- linear stability analysis;
- 2D / 3D nonlinear spectral simulation;
- self-similar rupture analysis.

This is theory / numerical analysis, not experimental validation.

## Q7 — Data / reproducibility

The paper reports:

- vapor recoil increases maximum growth rate and destabilizes the film;
- effective pressure stabilizes the system and delays rupture;
- interfacial energy transport has little effect on linear stability but changes wave speed;
- 3D spanwise perturbations can amplify vapor-recoil destabilization during evaporation;
- minimum film thickness scales as (t_r - t)^(1/2) when evaporation dominates;
- minimum film thickness scales as (t_r - t)^(1/3) when vapor recoil dominates.

These are model results, not phone-device measurements.

## Q8 — Evidence vs hypothesis

It demonstrates a current China capability in advanced nonlinear interfacial-stability mathematics.

It does not demonstrate:

- exact/group-invariant analytical solutions;
- direct UTVC performance prediction;
- experimental boundary validation;
- smartphone relevance by itself.

## Q9 — Real decision contribution

This paper makes a broad Russian mathematical-uniqueness thesis untenable.

The only defensible Russian residual is therefore methodological utility:
whether exact solutions and neutral-boundary constructions offer better interpretability or experiment-design leverage than other formal models.
