## Paper 10Q

### Q1. What problem is addressed?
How nanofluid behavior and nanostructure formation affect vapor generation and heat-transfer performance inside the finely porous wick of a loop heat pipe.

### Q2. Why does it matter?
LHP performance is controlled by coupled capillary transport, vapor generation, wick state and thermal resistance. These mechanisms are transferable to compact two-phase thermal-control design.

### Q3. What device / platform is studied?
An experimental loop heat pipe with fine-pore capillary structure.

### Q4. What is the technical approach?
The authors compare conventional working fluids and a NiO/water nanofluid under startup, variable heat-load and degradation tests.

### Q5. What are the main observations?
The nanofluid changes vapor-generation behavior and surface state inside the capillary structure and can improve heat-transfer performance over the tested range.

### Q6. What performance evidence is reported?
The paper reports higher maximum tested heat load and lower thermal resistance / evaporator-wall temperature for the nanofluid case than for the water baseline under the stated experimental conditions.

### Q7. What is the mechanism hypothesis?
Nanoparticles alter nucleation / vapor-generation conditions and generate nanoscale surface structures in the wick, changing local phase-change heat transfer.

### Q8. What is the strongest project-relevant insight?
UrFU remains experimentally active in LHP wick / vapor-generation physics in 2025, rather than being only a historical Soviet/Russian LHP lineage.

### Q9. What cannot be concluded?
The experiment does not establish smartphone UTVC dimensions, consumer-product reliability, long-life contamination behavior, or mass-production readiness.

### Q10. How should this affect the portfolio?
Treat UrFU as a current two-phase/LHP mechanism and device-research comparator. It strengthens the Russian capillary-device ecosystem but does not displace the more direct smartphone-oriented China baseline.
