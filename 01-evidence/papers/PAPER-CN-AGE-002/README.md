Title:
Research on a rapid prediction method for the service life of copper-water vapour chambers

Authors:
Xiaojun Guo; Yong Li; Wenjie Zhou; Yue Tian; Yang Yang; Fan Yang

Direct reported results:
- copper-water vapor chambers are accelerated-aged at 150–200 C and analyzed using failure analysis plus XPS/EDS;
- oxygen is identified as the primary cause of thermal-performance failure;
- wick chemistry evolves from copper toward Cu/Cu2O/CuO mixture, wettability changes from hydrophilic to hydrophobic and capillary pressure from positive to negative;
- around 1% wick-surface oxygen content corresponds to a predicted service life of 13 years or more at 80 C;
- oxygen content versus predicted actual service life follows a quadratic relationship with R2 = 0.98;
- the proposed "oxygen footprint" rapid life-prediction method reports approximately 8% prediction error;
- the study notes conventional accelerated life testing can still require roughly 40 days for a single experiment.

Boundary:
the 13-year value is model-converted / predicted service life rather than actual 13-year calendar operation;
the method is production-batch / chemistry dependent and is not a demonstrated smartphone remaining-life observer.
