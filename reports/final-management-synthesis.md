# 1. Executive Summary

## 1.1 Bottom line

Russia does **not** show a broad product-level advantage over China in smartphone thermal management.

China is stronger in:
- ultra-thin vapor-chamber / heat-spreader device engineering;
- copper-water product and manufacturing reliability;
- scalable wick / surface / microchannel engineering;
- product integration, supply chain and mobile implementation.

Russia's credible value is narrower and more complementary.

The most defensible collaboration opportunities are concentrated in:
1. **phase-change failure / boiling-crisis mechanism depth**;
2. **long-duration surface / capillary aging knowledge**;
3. **selected surface-process and wick-manufacturing methods**;
4. **extreme thin-film / interfacial instability physics**;
5. **supporting analytical modeling, diagnostics and surface chemistry**.

- **TsAGI / PNRPU** — aeroacoustic method reserve.

## 1.4 Recommended collaboration model

**Russian mechanism / diagnostics / specialized process knowledge**  
× **China ultra-thin device / manufacturing / reliability engineering**  
× **our smartphone architecture / boundary conditions / integration / control**  
→ **joint differentiated IP and product concepts**

The strategy is **complementary**, not substitutive.

# 2. Russia vs China Capability Landscape

## 2.1 Management heatmap

| Capability area | China baseline | Russia residual | Management judgment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ultra-thin VC / device integration | Strong | Weak | China dominant |
| Dryout / rewetting | Strong | narrow mechanism residual | Russia only on specific crisis interpretation |
| Wick / capillary engineering | Strong | selected process routes | complementary |
| Long-term two-phase reliability | Strong product/process QA | unusual multi-year capillary-aging evidence | complementary |
| Laser / biphilic surfaces | Strong | TPU process execution | challenger only |
| Thin-film / microfilm instability | Strong global competition | strong Kutateladze mechanism lineage | reserve |
| Microchannel cooling | Strong | current supporting nodes | China stronger |
| LHP | Strong current miniaturization | Russian foundational knowledge | watch |
| Aeroacoustics | Strong electronics-specific stack | Russian methods | watch |
| Materials / TIM / graphite | Strong | no clear Russian edge | do not position as Russia advantage |
| Thermal control / DVFS | Strong and crowded | no clear Russia edge | background only |
| Analytical stability / exact modeling | Strong globally | Russian analytical tradition | enabling reserve |
| Diagnostics / failure physics | Strong but fragmented | selected Russian depth | collaboration value |

## 2.2 Core conclusion

Russia should **not** be presented as a country-level leader in smartphone thermal hardware.

The correct framing is:

> Russia retains a small number of technically deep and potentially useful capabilities that may complement China's much stronger mobile-device engineering and productization ecosystem.

# 3. Priority Partner 1 — Kutateladze Institute / Lab 1.3

## 3.1 Institution and team

Institution:
**Kutateladze Institute of Thermophysics SB RAS**

Team:
**Lab 1.3 / low-temperature thermophysics line**

Key people:
- Alexander N. Pavlenko — senior scientific and outreach anchor;
- Anton V. Surtaev;
- Dmitry A. Shvetsov;
- V. I. Zhukov.

Leadership dossier:
[reports/leadership-dossiers/kutateladze-lab13.md](leadership-dossiers/kutateladze-lab13.md)

## 3.2 What they are genuinely strong at

Not generic "dryout detection".

The strongest residual is:
- boiling crisis;
- dry-spot formation and evolution;
- reversible vs irreversible crisis behavior;
- optical / IR mechanism diagnostics;
- modified mesh / capillary boiling.

Canonical capability:
`CAP-KUT-L13-DRYOUT-DIAGNOSTICS`

## 3.3 Why this matters for us

A smartphone thermal system rarely needs another generic over-temperature detector.

The potentially useful knowledge is:
- how a two-phase structure transitions from recoverable to unrecoverable state;
- what physical observables correspond to that transition;
- which design variables determine crisis onset / recovery;
- how to distinguish transport failure from generic package/interface drift.

## 3.4 Collaboration readiness

Pavlenko / Lab 1.3 currently has the strongest public collaboration-readiness signal:
- Huawei technical agreement history;
- Bel Huawei consulting evidence;
- earlier industrial cooperation;
- current collaboration with Xi'an Jiaotong University;
- RSF/NSFC activity;
- active multiphase / microelectronics-relevant programs.

## 3.5 China baseline

China is already strong in:
- capillary-fed dryout / rewetting;
- treated mesh;
- transient time-to-dryout / time-to-rewet;
- ultra-thin wick/device engineering;
- dielectric-fluid / chip phase-change work.

Shanghai Jiao Tong University materially strengthens this baseline through:
- dielectric-fluid evaporation in ultra-thin composite wick structures;
- near-junction / 3D-chip thermal management;
- dryout-mitigation microchannel boiling.

Therefore the Russia value must remain narrow:
**mechanism-specific crisis diagnostics and failure-state interpretation.**

## 3.6 Recommended collaboration scope

Best first collaboration question:

> Can Lab 1.3's reversible-to-irreversible crisis diagnostics provide mechanism labels, failure-boundary knowledge or design rules that remain useful under thin, sealed, phone-relevant copper-water conditions?

We should own:
- phone architecture;
- UTVC geometry;
- copper-water process;
- product telemetry;
- final control algorithm;
- product integration;
- foreground IP.

## 3.7 Main risks

- HFE/open/thin-layer evidence may not transfer to sealed copper-water UTVC;
- phone-scale device validation is absent;
- current Huawei scope is not public;
- willingness / staffing / IP terms require direct contact.

## 3.8 Management decision

**KEEP — PRIORITY 1**

# 4. Priority Partner 2 — MPEI

## 4.1 Institution and people

Institution:
**National Research University Moscow Power Engineering Institute**

Key people:
- Nikita S. Ivanov;
- Yuri A. Kuzma-Kichta;
- Maria M. Alyautdinova;
- Yuriy V. Lyulin;
- Alexey V. Dedov;
- separate current microchannel team.

Leadership dossier:
[reports/leadership-dossiers/mpei-heat-transfer.md](leadership-dossiers/mpei-heat-transfer.md)

## 4.2 Strongest capability

The key differentiator is **not generic VC reliability**.

It is:
- multi-year operation of engineered heat-transfer surfaces;
- capillary / imbibition state evolution;
- controlled wettability;
- engineering realization through external manufacturing / implementation partners.

Canonical capability:
`CAP-MPEI-LONGTERM-CAPILLARY-AGING`

## 4.3 Why the 42-month evidence matters

The important point is not that the device "worked for 42 months".

The useful research value is:
- actual calendar-time operation;
- post-operation surface / capillary state change;
- evidence that integral thermal behavior and local capillary state can evolve differently.

This may help define:
- degradation taxonomy;
- accelerated-aging labels;
- which surface/wetting indicators matter.

It does **not** prove:
- remaining-life estimation;
- online health monitoring;
- smartphone VC transfer.

## 4.4 Engineering breadth

MPEI is stronger than a single aging paper suggests.

Additional current evidence includes:
- Newfrost implementation/manufacturing chain;
- controlled-wettability thermosyphon work;
- SiC-coated microchannel research;
- MPEI + Skoltech additive-manufactured flat two-phase evaporator / loop thermosyphon.

Supporting capability:
`CAP-MPEI-AM-THERMOSYPHON`

## 4.5 China baseline

China is strong in:
- copper-water oxidation failure;
- lifetime prediction;
- aging test methods;
- product/process QA;
- mobile-scale thin two-phase devices.

SJTU further raises the baseline through:
- dielectric-fluid ultra-thin composite wick research;
- chip near-junction / 3D embedded thermal management;
- microchannel / phase-change work.

Therefore MPEI's residual is:
**actual long-duration engineered-surface / capillary-state aging evidence.**

## 4.6 Recommended collaboration scope

Best first collaboration question:

> Does MPEI's long-duration surface / capillary experience reveal degradation modes, labels or reliability metrics that are not already captured by oxygen / oxidation / process QA in phone-relevant copper-water structures?

We should own:
- phone geometry;
- copper-water process;
- manufacturing qualification;
- product reliability model;
- algorithms / telemetry;
- foreground IP.

## 4.7 Main risks

- R410A / thermosyphon scale differs from phone VC;
- gravity sensitivity;
- exact Newfrost role needs confirmation;
- no phone OEM proof.

## 4.8 Management decision

**KEEP — PRIORITY 2**

# 5. Priority Partner 3 — TPU

## 5.1 Institution and people

Institution:
**Tomsk Polytechnic University**

Key people:
- Dmitry V. Feoktistov;
- Evgeniya G. Orlova.

Leadership dossier:
[reports/leadership-dossiers/tpu-surface-process.md](leadership-dossiers/tpu-surface-process.md)

## 5.2 Strongest capability

TPU should not be described as "Russia's laser-surface advantage".

Generic laser/biphilic surface engineering is already crowded.

The retained value is:
- process execution;
- copper/metal surface patterning;
- wettability control;
- surface durability;
- sample/process development.

Canonical capability:
`CAP-TPU-LASER-WETTABILITY-PROCESS`

## 5.3 China baseline

China already has:
- direct laser modification of UTVC wick;
- biphilic/wettability structures;
- device-level UTVC verification;
- broad surface-process implementation.

Therefore TPU's useful question is:

> Can it fabricate a process that remains stable under thin-copper, vacuum, DI-water and sealed-VC constraints?

## 5.4 Recommended role

- process challenger;
- sample fabrication;
- second-source process exploration;
- bounded surface-pattern study.

## 5.5 Main risks

- strongest current IP is not smartphone-specific;
- vacuum / outgassing / DI-water compatibility unproven;
- phone-thickness manufacturability unknown;
- public OEM collaboration evidence is limited.

## 5.6 Management decision

**KEEP — PRIORITY 3 / CHALLENGER**

# 7. China Strong Baseline

## 7.1 Why this section matters

The Russia opportunity only makes sense against a strong China comparator.

The project should never imply that Russia is valuable because China "does not have" these capabilities.

## 7.2 Key China anchors

### Shanghai Jiao Tong University

Cross-cutting baseline:
- micro/nano phase change;
- dielectric-fluid evaporation;
- ultra-thin composite wick;
- near-junction chip thermal management;
- 3D embedded thermal management;
- microchannel flow boiling.

### Guangdong University of Technology

Baseline:
- capillary-fed boiling;
- dryout / rewetting;
- hydrophilicity degradation.

### East China Jiaotong University

Baseline:
- laser-modified UTVC wick;
- device-level capillary / thermal verification.

### China copper-water VC reliability ecosystem

Baseline:
- oxidation failure;
- oxygen/process control;
- lifetime prediction;
- pre-encapsulation aging methods;
- product/manufacturing integration.

## 7.3 Management takeaway

China is the stronger product-engineering baseline.

Russian value must therefore be:
- specific;
- complementary;
- mechanism- or process-deep;
- difficult to replace with routine domestic engineering.

# 9. Collaboration Architecture

## 9.1 Recommended model

### Russia contributes

- mechanism depth;
- failure physics;
- specialized diagnostics;
- long-duration surface-aging knowledge;
- niche process / surface / wick methods;
- selected analytical support.

### China contributes

- ultra-thin device engineering;
- manufacturing process;
- supply chain;
- reliability qualification;
- mature VC / microchannel implementation.

### We should own

- smartphone boundary conditions;
- product architecture;
- target geometry;
- integration constraints;
- telemetry / software / controller;
- product-level validation;
- foreground IP strategy.

## 9.2 Collaboration principle

Do not ask:
> "Can the Russian partner build a better smartphone thermal system for us?"

Ask:
> "Which specialized capability do they have that can improve a specific weakness or uncertainty in our own smartphone thermal architecture?"
