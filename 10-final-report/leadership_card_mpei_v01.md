# 1. Product problem

Phone two-phase cooling reliability is normally judged through:
- thermal resistance;
- leak/process control;
- oxidation;
- accelerated aging.

But a surface may lose **capillary / liquid-supply health** before nominal thermal resistance visibly fails.

The product question is:

> Can an early surface/capillary health indicator predict later loss of phone dryout margin before conventional thermal-resistance failure becomes visible?

# 6. Phone-transfer gap

The current hierarchy is not a phone geometry.

Known/reference-scale issues:
- hierarchical groove radius around ~100 μm;
- phone UTVC internal channel reference ~0.2 mm;
- current thermosyphon heat flux far below localized phone-hotspot regime;
- historical coating thickness partly known, but current as-built transfer stack/yield remains partner data;
- direct permeability of the exact phone-transfer coupon is missing;
- copper / DI water / vacuum / sealing / cycling remains unproven.

The biggest risk is:

> long-life capability is real, but only in a geometry/application class that cannot be scaled into a modern phone VC.

# 8. Success / Kill

## Internal Stage-0 promotion target

Must:
- fit **<=150 μm** transfer ceiling or replace another structural element;
- survive DI water + VC process;
- retain stable capillary/permeability function;
- repeat across **n>=3**.

Then achieve at least one versus strong reference M3:

- **>=15% lower evaporator thermal resistance; OR**
- **>=20% higher dryout/capillary limit; OR**
- **>=20% faster rewetting.**

For the reliability thesis, MPEI must additionally show that the capillary/surface metric provides useful predictive value for future dryout-margin degradation beyond existing domestic oxidation/Rth metrics.

**These are internal Stage-0 targets, not literature claims.**

## Kill / reframe

Kill/reframe phone thesis if:
- domestic oxygen/oxidation metrics explain aging equally well;
- capillary drift does not correlate with dryout margin;
- hierarchy cannot enter UTVC geometry budget;
- permeability/liquid supply collapses with scale-down;
- high-flux benefit disappears;
- copper/process/cycling is unstable;
- strong modern reference matches scaled hierarchy.

If durability remains valuable but geometry fails:
**reframe to long-life thermosyphon / reliability know-how.**
