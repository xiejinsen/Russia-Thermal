# Russia Institution Landscape — First Scan

Status: preliminary seed map. No overall ranking.

## 1. Kutateladze Institute of Thermophysics SB RAS — Novosibirsk

### Why it matters
**Source fact:** the institute describes itself as a leading center in heat-transfer theory and physical hydro-gas dynamics, with work on boiling, condensation, phase-change heat/mass transfer and dispersed flows.

Official overview:
https://www.itp.nsc.ru/about.html

### High-relevance evidence

#### A. Boiling / evaporation / dielectric liquids / modified surfaces
The Low-Temperature Thermophysics Laboratory lists:
- heat-transfer enhancement during boiling and evaporation;
- modified surfaces;
- thin layers of dielectric liquids;
- a 2023–2025 RSF project on these topics.

Official lab page:
https://www.itp.nsc.ru/structura/nauchnye_porazdeleniya/13_laboratoriya_nizkotemperaturnoy_teplofiziki.html

2025 publication list includes a review explicitly on enhanced boiling/evaporation of dielectric fluids for immersion cooling of electronics:
https://www.itp.nsc.ru/structura/nauchnye_porazdeleniya/2025_lab1.3.html

#### B. Direct industrial signal: Huawei
**Source fact:** the official lab page records a 2021–2023 scientific/technical agreement with Huawei-related entities on boiling heat-transfer enhancement and a 2024–2026 consulting agreement with Bel Huawei Technologies.

This is unusually important evidence because it validates industrial interest in the exact capability family we are exploring.

Source:
https://www.itp.nsc.ru/lmpt/?lang=en&page_id=1257

#### C. Microchannel / dielectric-liquid boiling
Ronshin, Dementiev and Chinnov studied FC-72 boiling in a ~66 μm-height slit microchannel.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1134/S086986432206018X

#### D. Synthetic-jet heat transfer
Lemanov, Pakhomov and Terekhov experimentally/numerically studied impinging synthetic-jet heat transfer.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.31857/S0040364423020126

### Candidate researchers / sub-lines
- Aleksandr N. Pavlenko — boiling/evaporation, modified surfaces, multiphase heat transfer
- Fedor V. Ronshin — microchannel two-phase flow / boiling
- Evgeny A. Chinnov — multiphase flow / films / microchannels
- V. V. Lemanov — synthetic jets / flow
- M. A. Pakhomov — flow & heat-transfer modeling
- V. I. Terekhov — convection / jet heat transfer

Current-role verification and publication-network analysis remain pending.

### Mobile-transfer hypotheses
H1. Modified capillary/porous boiling surfaces could contribute to ultra-thin two-phase devices.
H2. Microchannel dielectric-boiling knowledge could transfer to localized high-flux phone cooling, but pump/sealing complexity is a major barrier.
H3. Synthetic-jet expertise is directly relevant to solid-state/zero-net-mass-flow active cooling.
H4. Existing Huawei interaction may mean the most obvious collaboration ideas are already partly explored; differentiation/IP whitespace must be checked.

## 2. Institute of Thermal Physics, Ural Branch RAS — Yekaterinburg

Official site:
https://itpuran.ru/

Official institution identity/contact:
https://itpuran.ru/index.php/contactus

### Distinctive capability
Strong lineage in **loop heat pipes (LHPs)** and other two-phase heat-transfer devices.

The institute's current structure lists Yu. F. Maydanik in the Laboratory of Heat-Transfer Devices:
https://itpuran.ru/index.php/about/struktura-instituta

### Recent evidence
2025: Maydanik, Vershinin, Chernysheva studied an LHP with low-temperature working fluids:
https://doi.org/10.7868/S3034610X25060094

2024: long flexible LHP:
https://doi.org/10.1134/S0018151X24700044

2022: flat-evaporator LHP work:
https://doi.org/10.31857/S0040364422030127

### Candidate researchers
- Yu. F. Maydanik
- M. A. Chernysheva
- S. V. Vershinin

### Mobile-transfer hypotheses
H1. LHP architecture may offer a route to spatially decouple small moving hotspots from heat-rejection zones.
H2. Their multi-source / flat-evaporator / flexible-device experience may be more valuable than simply importing a conventional spacecraft LHP.
H3. Main transfer risks are thickness, startup behavior, orientation, working fluid, manufacturing and phone-scale condenser design.

## 7. St. Petersburg State University — Electrophysics / ionic wind

### Evidence
The SPbU Research and Educational Center “Electrophysics” has a continuing electrohydrodynamic / ionic-wind modeling line. COMSOL Conference 2024 lists “The Fast Model for Ionic Wind Simulation” by A. Samusenko, Yu. Stishkov and P. Zhidkova.

Source:
https://www.comsol.com/papers-presentations

A recent Russian-language publication also continues the electric-wind modeling line; earlier work explicitly modeled plate cooling by ionic wind.

### Candidate people
- A. V. Samusenko
- P. S. Zhidkova
- Yu. Stishkov

### Mobile-transfer hypothesis
This is an **exploratory** candidate for solid-state air movement. No recent direct smartphone-cooling demonstration has yet been verified.

Key feasibility questions:
- required voltage;
- ozone/chemistry;
- electrode lifetime/contamination;
- net airflow per watt;
- package thickness;
- EMC/safety.

## Early capability pattern

**Cross-source observation:** the most distinctive Russian signals found in the first scan are not “smartphone cooling products.” They are deeper capabilities in:
- phase-change / boiling physics;
- capillary/two-phase heat transport;
- synthetic jets;
- EHD/ionic wind;
- aeroacoustics and fan-noise measurement.

This is encouraging for the project's strategy: mine Russian **mechanism depth**, then combine it with Chinese/mobile productization strengths.

- No collaboration priority ordering yet.
- No evidence yet that aeroacoustic methods scale cleanly to sub-centimeter phone fans.
- No evidence yet that Russian EHD work is product-ready.
