## Findings

### 1. Russian exact/stability capability is real

PAPER-RU-MODEL-001:
- derives a current exact group-nature solution for coupled evaporative convection;
- uses nonlinear concentration-dependent surface tension;
- can impose experimental gas flow as an integral condition to infer other operating parameters;
- reports qualitative agreement with experiment;
- is positioned as a benchmark / preliminary optimization tool.

PAPER-RU-MODEL-002:
- uses an exact thermosolutal base state plus spectral stability analysis;
- constructs explicit neutral curves and instability domains;
- reports an equal-load critical wave number of 2.48 and critical Grashof number of 0.08;
- shows top heating can create a finite stable parameter interval;
- identifies oscillatory cellular instability modes.

### 2. Broad Russian mathematical uniqueness remains refuted

PAPER-CN-MODEL-001 independently demonstrates:
- 3D nonlinear evaporating/condensing-film modeling;
- linear stability analysis;
- vapor-recoil / effective-pressure competition;
- nonlinear rupture evolution;
- self-similar thinning laws.

The methodology is different from the Russian exact/group-invariant school, but broad interfacial-stability mathematics is clearly not unique to Russia.

### 3. Product-relevant global modeling is much closer to the user target

PAPER-GLOBAL-UTVC-MODEL-001 directly targets mobile-electronics UTVCs and reports:
- 4–5 orders lower computational cost than CFD;
- >10% vapor-pressure-drop prediction difference below 150 micrometers when temperature-dependent vapor density is included;
- about 5.3x Qmax increase in the analyzed mesh-wick case when vapor-core thickness increases from 50 to 90 micrometers;
- hybrid-wick Qmax around 4.84 W in the analyzed case;
- a strong shift toward vapor-flow resistance as vapor-core thickness decreases.

This means "analytical / fast / interpretable model" is not itself a Russian strategic control point.
