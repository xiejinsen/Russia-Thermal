### Track C — Russia translation / industry-adoption layer

Purpose:
avoid treating academic strength as collaboration readiness.

Investigate:
- industry-funded projects;
- licensing / patents with implementation evidence;
- prototypes / demonstrators;
- corporate or government technical partners;
- production or pilot-line evidence;
- equipment/material supply capability;
- whether a technology remains academic-only.

Focus:
only technologies in or adjacent to the surviving portfolio.

Output:
- academic → prototype → industry translation map;
- manufacturability / scale-up evidence;
- collaboration-risk flags.

### Track D — Strong China counterpart deepening

Purpose:
make sure Russia residual differentiation survives not just academic comparison, but the strongest domestic engineering capability.

Deepen by exact control point:
- irreversible dryout / rewetting;
- multi-year surface / wick aging and reliability health indicators;
- thin-film instability / hybrid active cooling;
- analytical / reduced-order failure-boundary modeling;
- surface-process / laser / biphilic sealed-VC compatibility.

Compare:
- academic institutions;
- top domestic engineering/product capability where public evidence exists;
- device geometry and working-fluid realism;
- reliability/manufacturing maturity.

Output:
- upgraded China mirror;
- stronger Kill/Keep boundaries;
- revised Russia residual-control-point wording if needed.

### Track E — Stage-0 measurement and execution readiness

Purpose:
turn the research into an executable experiment before final leadership recommendation.

Close:
- exact coupon drawings;
- measurement stack;
- uncertainty / repeatability plan;
- dry-spot / capillary / permeability metrics;
- comparator definition;
- test order;
- data format;
- success / Kill logic;
- which measurements could use SPbPU/JIHT-style capabilities;
- background vs foreground IP questions.

Output:
- execution-ready Stage-0 package;
- no ambiguity about what evidence changes the decision.
