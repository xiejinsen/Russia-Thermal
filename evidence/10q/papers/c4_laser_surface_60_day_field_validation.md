# C4 — Laser-modified heat-transfer surface: 60-day field validation

>
> Navigation: [Paper 10Q Index](README.md) · [10Q Method](../../mobile_thermal_insight_10q_method.md)

**[A New Approach to Reducing Slagging Based on Laser Modification of Heating Surfaces: Field Tests and Mathematical Modeling](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fuel.2024.133778)** — D.V. Feoktistov, D.O. Glushkov, K.K. Paushkina, E.G. Orlova *et al.* — *Fuel*, 2025.

**Review status:** PUBLISHER FULL-RECORD / DECISION-RELEVANT REVIEW.

### Q1 — problem + target mapping
Problem:
can laser-modified heat-transfer surfaces retain a useful functional state in a real dirty thermal system rather than only in short laboratory tests?

Target mapping:
**indirect to phone thermal**, but directly relevant to TPU **process durability / scale-up / real-equipment execution**.

### Q2 — novelty / new-regime relevance
The decision-relevant novelty for this project is not anti-slagging itself.

It is:
> the same TPU laser-surface manufacturing family was exercised in a prolonged operating thermal system.

This is a translation signal, not a mobile-cooling mechanism proof.

### Q3 — falsifiable hypothesis
Hypothesis:
laser-created microchannel/passivated surface states retain lower deposit adhesion under prolonged real boiler exposure than conventional ground steel surfaces.

Falsifier:
no sustained field difference after long exposure.

### Q4 — research lineage / competing route
Same Feoktistov/Orlova laser-surface lineage used in:
- wettability control;
- boiling enhancement;
- biphilic/roughness work;
- RU2812668 laser-process IP.

Competing route:
conventional surface preparation / untreated or ground metal.

### Q5 — mechanism / control point
Control variables:
- nanosecond laser surface modification;
- microchannel / anisotropic texture;
- oxide/passivated state;
- steel substrate.

Mechanism in this paper:
reduced adhesion/deposition of combustion products.

For Russia-Thermal, the transferable control point is **surface-process robustness**, not the anti-slagging mechanism.

### Q6 — experiment design
Field test:
- operating brown-coal boiler;
- ~400 kW;
- ~60 days;
- AISI 310S coupons ~25 × 25 × 2 mm;
- laser-textured versus conventional surface states;
- microscopy, XRF, mass/deposit analysis;
- thermal modeling.

This is much stronger environmental realism than a short coupon-only laboratory durability test.

### Q7 — data / artifact / reproducibility
Source reports:
- up to ~60% lower slagging intensity for the microchannel texture;
- prolonged field survival;
- model-based heat-transfer implications.

Reproducibility:
MEDIUM for the published industrial environment.

For phone transfer:
LOW, because substrate/process stack and environment differ radically.

### Q8 — evidence vs hypothesis
**Measured / directly supported**
- field exposure;
- surface state;
- deposit behavior;
- multi-week survival.

**Not supported**
- copper sealed-VC durability;
- vacuum stability;
- outgassing;
- confined two-phase behavior;
- mobile manufacturing yield.

### Q9 — contribution to project decision
Belief changed:
> TPU is not merely a laboratory surface-physics group; it has evidence of taking laser-modified thermal surfaces into prolonged real-equipment field testing.

What is still killed:
> field validation in a boiler does not prove phone-product transfer.

So the correct upgrade is:
**process-translation confidence ↑**, not Stage-0 ranking.

### Q10 — next action
Keep TPU at Stage-0 #3.

Use the paper to ask more demanding partner questions:
- what laser equipment / fixture scale was used for field coupons?
- process repeatability / throughput?
- whether the same process can be applied to copper at phone-scale thickness;
- whether low-organic laser-only surfaces can survive vacuum/sealed-water processing.

Do not spend more public-search effort looking for generic "can TPU laser process survive outside the lab?" — this paper already answers that at least for a non-mobile field environment.

**Decision:** KEEP / PROCESS-TRANSLATION UPGRADE / PHONE-TRANSFER UNCHANGED.
