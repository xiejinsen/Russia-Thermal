# Film/Droplet Engineering Addendum v0.2

Last updated: 2026-10-03

## Mechanism-to-system gap

The patent lineage establishes strong mechanism ownership but also reveals a system gap.

RU2860581 cites RU2822416 as closest prior art:
https://patents.google.com/patent/RU2860581C1/en

The newer device still relies on:
- a micro/mini-channel;
- gas-liquid nozzles;
- controlled gas and liquid delivery;
- staged introduction of gas, film and droplets.

Older Kabov experimental work demonstrates that shear-driven film cooling can achieve very high local heat fluxes, but representative rigs include external gas/liquid handling hardware.

2007 foundation:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijheatfluidflow.2006.05.010

2017 microgroove experiment:
https://doi.org/10.1051/epjconf/201715900016

The 2017 setup included a high-pressure N2 source, FC-72 feed, syringe pump, membrane pump, separator/recovery path and thermoelectric inlet conditioning.

## Engineering conclusion

The limiting smartphone question is not:
> Can a shear-driven film remove heat?

It can.

The question is:
> Can the fluid-management system be reduced enough that its total power and volume are better than a microfan or micropump loop?

## Required quantitative data for next round

Extract or experimentally estimate:
- superficial gas velocity;
- gas volumetric flow;
- liquid mass flow;
- total pressure drop;
- pump/blower efficiency;
- condenser duty;
- separator volume;
- working-fluid inventory;
- transient response.

Until these are known, high CHF figures should **not** be used as evidence of smartphone superiority.
