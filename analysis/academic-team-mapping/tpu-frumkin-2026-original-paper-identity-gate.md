## Mechanism and actual observations

Setup: experimental water droplet evaporation on roughness-engineered mixed-wettability aluminum-magnesium surfaces under external heating. Laser processing plus surface-chemistry tuning using hydrocarbon-pyrolysis-derived alkyl groups controls texture and local hydrophobic/hydrophilic patterns. Diagnostics in article abstract include high-speed imaging, PIV, PLIF and embedded near-surface small thermocouples (around 500 micrometres beneath surface); compares local temperature fields and evaporation behavior.

The institute report gives heating temperature program 20–300 °C; journal abstract indexed experimental range 80–300 °C, so do not silently claim identical protocols/temperature range. Indexed abstract reports 30–45% superhydrophobic-area cases: at 80 °C, local temperature reduction 79–81% greater than polished control despite 29–74% lower evaporation rate, and a larger enhancement for selected high-roughness configurations at moderate temperatures (up to 20× **relative local temperature-drop metric**). Institute press report calls 6× improvement in one particular selected comparison. These are **different normalized local metrics/conditions** and cannot be conflated. The report also indicates a shift in best performance temperature from about 140 to 160 °C. None is the thermal resistance, energy efficiency, CHF or power improvement of a sealed phone chip cooler.

Value as a research technique: disentangles chemical wettability contrast from roughness; supports measurement of contact-line/Marangoni-convection effects beyond simple evaporation mass loss. It is *not* a verified capillary-porous sealed copper-water wick or ultra-thin VC process.
