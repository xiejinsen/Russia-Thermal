# 1. Pavlenko / Kutateladze — experiment-level hardening

## 1.1 Current experimental platform

New experiment-level facts from current/public Kutateladze work:

- HFE-7100 boiling is studied in a **vacuum-capable heat-transfer setup**;
- cylindrical stainless-steel working chamber:
  - inner diameter ~120 mm;
  - height ~300 mm;
  - wall thickness ~1 mm;
- bottom heating uses a ~3 kW tubular heater with a ~30 mm copper spreader plate;
- pressure above the liquid was varied at about **50 / 100 / 150 kPa**;
- HFE-7100 liquid-layer height was varied from about **1.5 to 35 mm**;
- working liquid was carefully degassed by boiling at reduced pressure;
- high-speed video is used during experiments;
- separate current crisis studies use high-speed thermography;
- current capillary-porous coating studies include **additively manufactured / 3D-printed stainless-steel and bronze structures**;
- current coating/crisis work is supported by **Russian Science Foundation grant 23-19-00245**.

## 1.2 Current dry-spot / crisis diagnostic depth

The 2025/2026 Surtaev–Malakhov–Perminov–Polovnikov–Pavlenko work confirms:
- synchronized high-speed IR + reflected-light / internal-reflection-style phase visualization;
- CNN-assisted dry-spot segmentation;
- dry-spot density;
- contact-line length;
- size distribution;
- reversible and irreversible dry-spot behavior;
- measured irreversible-dry-spot propagation;
- boiling curves averaged over multiple experimental runs;
- atmospheric-pressure HFE-7100 / Novec 649 comparison.

Public quantitative result:
- maximum HTC and CHF for HFE-7100 are higher than Novec 649 by about **1.47×** and **1.56×** respectively in that tested system.

Related 2026 silicon-substrate work reports:
- HFE-7100 CHF ~183 kW/m²;
- Novec 649 CHF ~151 kW/m²;
- irreversible dry-spot propagation ~10.4 mm/s for HFE-7100;
- ~6.8 mm/s for Novec 649.

These numbers are useful as **diagnostic capability evidence**, not phone-product targets.

The group owns at least the academic process chain for:

## 2.2 Exact current hierarchy / test scale

Current 2025/2026 thermosyphon paper reports:
- longitudinal grooves of about **0.1 mm radius**;
- Al2O3 nanoparticles about **100–200 nm**;
- experimental section about **100 mm long**;
- stainless-steel tube about **38 × 3 mm**;
- evaporator inclination tested around **0–30°**;
- heat-flux range about **200–1700 W/m²**;
- thermal resistance reduction about **2.4–3.0×** versus smooth thermosyphon in that system;
- model agreement within about **20%**.

### Important correction

That heat-flux range is **orders of magnitude below phone hotspot heat flux**.

This means:
- the 42-month evidence is excellent as an **aging / surface-state history**;
- it is weak as direct high-flux phone evidence.

The old 0.2 mm water microchannel / CHF lineage therefore remains essential to the transfer argument.

## 2.3 42-month evidence remains unusual

The 2026 aging study confirms:
- actual **42-month** campaign;
- R410A working fluid;
- repeated thermal cycling / phase transitions;
- monthly calendar-time steady-state test series;
- representative heat load ~100 W;
- modified-system Rth ~0.015 K/W;
- approximately 3× lower Rth than smooth reference in the reported configuration;
- post-operation morphology / porous-matrix integrity / residual capillary behavior were examined.

## 2.4 New translation evidence — industrial implementation

# 3. Kabov / Chinnov — prototype and system-level hardening

## 3.1 Existing experimental-industrial prototype is stronger than previously captured

A 2017 experimental model of a forced-circulation evaporative cooling system for high-performance electronics reports:

- closed gas and liquid loops;
- slot channel height about **1.0 mm** and width about **11 mm**;
- channel height adjustable roughly **0.1–2.0 mm**;
- local heater **10 × 10 mm**;
- working liquids including water / ethanol / HFE-7100;
- prototype test at maximum reported load used **distilled water + purified air**;
- liquid flow about **100–120 ml/min**;
- gas flow about **45–50 l/min**;
- external coolant around **23–25°C**;
- reported heat-flux capability up to about **1000 W/cm²**;
- maximum-load continuous test about **50 min**;
- reported local heater surface temperature <=~140°C;
- no stationary dry spots visually observed up to the reported maximum in that test.

Earlier program history also reports:
- removal up to ~870 W/cm² in prior experiments;
- stable operation across channel height about **0.17–2.0 mm**;
- orientation tests over a broad angular range;
- government-funded projects for high-performance processors / LED cooling;
- experimental sample / prototype development.

## 3.2 Current mechanism line remains active

2023 institute result on gas-sheared films reports:
- FC-72 film in horizontal microchannels;
- increasing gas and liquid flow increases CHF / rupture threshold;
- gas-sheared film CHF up to about **1.5×** falling-film case;
- longitudinal microfins of ~0.3 and 0.5 mm height raise CHF about **20–30%** via capillary rewetting.

Current 2025/2026 activity also includes:
- extreme-confinement flow-pattern work;
- Kabov–Lavrentyev microelectronics modeling;
- RU2860581C1 staged gas/drop/film electronics cooling patent.

## 3.3 What this changes

Previous wording risked making Kabov sound mainly like a mechanism/theory reserve.

New evidence supports a stronger statement:
> **Kabov has real system/prototype history in electronic cooling, not only film-physics papers.**

This modestly upgrades:
- execution confidence;
- translation lineage;
- partner readiness.

## 3.4 What does not improve

The same prototype data make the phone challenge even clearer:
- 45–50 l/min gas flow is extremely large for a smartphone;
- 100–120 ml/min liquid circulation is an active-loop burden;
- 1 mm-class channel and external condenser/separator/pumps are far from phone volume;
- continuous maximum-load validation was about 50 min, not product lifetime;
- very high heat-flux capability does not equal system efficiency.

### Decision

Kabov remains **High-risk Mechanism/IP Reserve**.

But the next research question changes from:
> can the team build such a system?

to:
> can the historical high-flow architecture be transformed into a radically lower-flow / lower-volume phone architecture without losing the failure-boundary advantage?

Partner request should ask for:
- latest prototype/system schematic;
- minimum demonstrated gas flow for stable film transport;
- minimum liquid inventory/flow;
- pump/compressor power;
- condenser/separator volume;
- acoustic data if any;
- current smallest channel / heater footprint;
- recent prototype status after RU2860581.
